PC platform hardening

Pause is presenter-owned; the sim never learns it exists. The accumulator simply is not fed while paused — no burst of catch-up steps on resume, however long the menu was open (L3 holds both ways). Rebinding is live: Settings.actionForCode() replaced the old static keymap, so a rebind takes effect on the very next keypress. Settings persist to localStorage through exactly two lines in the whole project allowed to throw on storage access, both wrapped — a private tab or disabled storage degrades to "play, but don't remember," never a crash. Reduced motion thins particles to ~35% but damps camera shake to 15%, not zero: shake is the vestibular-disorder trigger and gets the harder cut; a near-silent kick still confirms a hit landed.

JUMP_VEL is −5.55, not −5.4. The continuous apex formula v²/2g does not describe a 60 Hz integrator that applies gravity on the same tick as the impulse. Solving it gives −5.4 and a jump that measures 45.9 px — short of the three tiles specified. The discrete solution −5.55 measures 48.6 px over an 18-frame rise with a symmetric 18-frame fall.

The figure

The body skeleton lives beside the weapon poses because they have to agree about where the shoulder is. Both reach the screen through Combat.pointToWorld — the transform the sim tests hitboxes with — so the arm drawn on the body, the arm in a swing, and the box that swing tests are one piece of geometry. Mid-swing the drawn hand is the baked hand, asserted by object identity rather than by comparing numbers. The gait is driven by distance travelled, never a timer: 60 px per two-step cycle, one footfall every 12 frames at run speed, matching the step event. A time-driven cycle slides the feet the moment speed changes.
Two bugs no other assertion could see. Crouch dropped the hips and left the legs at standing angles, floating the character 5 px off the floor. And the carried sword — 16 px past the elbow, not 11 past the hand — buried its tip in the ground at rest and reached 25 px deep on the run back-swing. Nothing else in the project looks at where the DRAWING puts things, so both now have assertions: feet on the ground line in every grounded stance, and the blade inside the baked swing envelope across a full gait sweep of every state.

Watch companion — a static mockup, not a live view

CINDER_LOOP_WATCH_MOCKUP.html is a standalone file, not part of cinder-loop.html, not built by build.py, no gate entry. Three real interpretations of "watch companion" were named and put to a direct choice before anything was built — a static mockup, a live view over a new local network bridge (a deliberate exception to L2's zero-network stance for one optional feature), or a native Wear OS app in Kotlin (a wholesale separate project, different language and toolchain). The static mockup was chosen. The game is single-file, zero-network, no server by design, and Wear OS has no standard path to sideload an arbitrary HTML page — so a live version is not a small feature on top of what exists, it is a data bridge that does not exist in any form yet.

Every value on the face — hearts, the run timer's 04:12 starting point, biome name, room count — is a fixed sample; there is nothing to read from. The one genuinely live element is the timer's own tick (real elapsed time since load), labelled as exactly that. Sized to the Galaxy Watch6 Classic's actual native resolution, 480×480 round, not a guess. Verified in real Chromium at that exact size: zero console errors, correct dimensions, the timer observed actually advancing. Not verified on physical Wear OS hardware — no device was available, and there is no standard way to get this file onto one in the first place — stated rather than implied to be fine, the same discipline as the frame-meter caveat elsewhere on this page.

The stat contract, real pickups, and weapon scaling (v0.2.9)

Also not a new locked decision. D2 was specified in full at this project's very start — three colours, a pickup grants +1 to a chosen stat and +HP only if that stat is dominant, a weapon scales off the larger of its two named colours. This session is the first that actually builds it, the same designed→measured→implemented→ tested shape as v0.2.8, no judged panel needed.

Read precisely rather than assumed. "+HP" reuses D8's own vocabulary — meta currency already buys "+max HP" permanently, so the within-run version reads as the same concept. "Dominant" is read as STRICTLY the sole highest stat: catching up to exactly tie the leader grants no HP, only overtaking it does, proven directly rather than left to guesswork. The very first pickup of any run is unconditionally dominant (1,1,1 → 2,1,1 has nothing to tie against), which is correct, not a loophole — the anti-death-spiral property only has work to do once stats have actually diverged.

One real, named simplification. "Dual choices weighted toward the two lowest stats" implies choosing between two spatial options; 50-gen.js's pickups are single points, and reshaping an already-shipped, fairness-audited generator was real scope not taken on here. The weighting is applied instead at the moment of collection (pickStatColour, 30-player.js): the current sole leader is weighted down, never excluded — confirmed over 3000 real seeded draws that it still gets picked sometimes, just measurably less than either trailing stat, and that a genuine three-way tie stays roughly uniform.

Weapon scaling, wired for real. WEAPONS (10-data.js) already named two colours per weapon since v0.2.8; a new Combat.weaponScale now reads the LARGER of the two — never their sum, never an unrelated third colour, both proven directly — and multiplies the move's own baked damage by 1 + (statValue − 1) × STAT_SCALE_PER_POINT, rounded once, at the point of application, so every damage number this game shows stays a clean integer. STAT_SCALE_PER_POINT (0.15) is named in its own comment as a design judgment, not a measurement — the same discipline as GEN_MIN_FIGHT_TILES. A weapon with no registered colours falls back to the highest of all three stats, the fallback D2 names explicitly, proven with an unregistered weapon id.

A real bug, caught by writing the right assertion, not by luck. The first draft of the pickup-collision tests never actually drove the player into the pickup — it sat edge-touching the spawn box (aabb() needs real overlap, not touching edges), so nothing was ever collected. Two of the three pickup tests passed anyway, for the wrong reason: "collecting an already-collected pickup grants nothing more" and "it becomes uncollected after a reset" are both trivially true if nothing was ever collected in the first place — the same "a check quietly becomes a no-op" failure 35-rig.js's own audit exists to guard against, just found in the harness this time. Fixed by driving real movement into the pickup and adding an explicit "collection actually happened" assertion before each negative claim.

What was deliberately not done: no spatial dual-choice UI — see the simplification above. Weapon equipping/switching still has no player-facing path (D4, not D2). 65-meta.js's permanent, cross-run progression is untouched; D2's stats are explicitly WITHIN one run, reset by resetTransient(), an interim reading of "a run boundary" until 60-run.js defines one more precisely. The HUD's own heart-meter rendering still hardcodes CFG.MAX_HP for its draw loop — sim-level growth is real and tested, the visual is deferred, the same shape as the boss's own health-bar deferral.

Slam impact — the ground-pound finally hits something (v0.2.11)

Unlike Warmaul/Thornspear, D9 named an open weapon COUNT but slam-as-attack has only one real fork (universal vs. per-weapon), and D7's own "a weapon owns no numbers of its own" already argues against per-weapon for a movement-triggered shockwave that was never a swing. A connecting slam requests CFG.HITSTOP_HEAVY instead of the unconditional landing 6, the same "biggest hits get the biggest freeze" rule every weapon's own heavy follows.

The architecture question, decided by re-reading existing rules. Teaching it a second knockback model would be exactly the "two places that subtract from hp" failure this project's own rule warns against. Ledge grab / mantle — real spatial reasoning, not a velocity clamp (v0.2.12)

No judged panel — the same reasoning that skipped one for wall interaction and slam impact. Catch a wall's own top edge while falling into it, climb with jump, drop with down: genre-standard shape, not open creative space. What made THIS feature different from either precedent: wall slide/jump are velocity clamps that need no world-knowledge beyond "touching a wall right now," but a ledge grab is a real claim about the tilemap — the wall has to actually run out, within reach, into a surface the player can stand on. detectLedge() was written as its own function and proven against three constructed test worlds (a clean ledge, a wall that never ends within reach, a ledge with no headroom to climb into) before it was ever wired into update() — the same bake-before-ship discipline every rig move already follows, applied to collision geometry instead of a pose.

The numbers: grab condition is airborne, touching a wall, holding INTO it, vy ≥ 0, not locked out — checked BEFORE wall slide's own clamp, since a genuine ledge takes priority over merely sliding past the same wall. Climb input is jump (genre-standard, not a new "up" input this project doesn't otherwise use anywhere). Release is down, or a 90-frame (LEDGE_GRAB_MAX_HANG) timeout. Re-grab lockout is 10 frames (LEDGE_GRAB_LOCKOUT), stopping the ledge just climbed off from instantly re-catching on the way up. While hanging, velocity is pinned to zero every tick — the wall IS the support, the same way a ground tile is.

Three real bugs, caught by hand before any test ever ran. One: the first climb draft nudged the body a fixed 6px off the wall face rather than anchoring to the tile grid — traced by hand that this left the body straddling two tile columns instead of standing fully on the ledge. Fixed by anchoring directly to the tile boundary detectLedge() had already proven has headroom. Two: immediately after climbing, state read fall and onGround read false for exactly one tick despite the body already sitting correctly — the same class of bug already fixed once for roll's own start frame: move()'s Y-step never runs at all when vy is exactly 0. Fixed the same way, b.vy += CFG.GRAVITY on the transition tick. Three: finish()'s own state classifier would have silently overwritten state = 'ledgeGrab' back to 'wallSlide' the same tick it was set, since its exclusion list didn't yet know the state existed — caught by re-reading finish() before ever running a test.

A dedicated adversarial pass, run the same way as every feature this session, stayed five-for-five — four more real problems, three substantive. One: a crouched approach that then climbs never stood the body up: b.h stayed at the crouched 12px while b.y was computed for the standing 22px ledge position, leaving the feet ~10px above the real surface, self-healing only several ticks later once the ordinary in-air auto-uncrouch caught up. Fixed by standing the body up unconditionally on the climb transition; unlike the ground jump's own crouch-cancel (which asks permission via a real canStand() check), detectLedge() had already proven the clearance at the specific landing spot. Two: a hazard hit couldn't knock a hanging player free — the ledgeGrab block's own per-tick "pin velocity to zero" ran again the very next tick, before move() ever applied the hurt knockback. Fixed in Player.prototype.hurt(): a hit now releases the hang immediately, the same way every other interruption in this game already takes priority over holding still. Three: the "touching but not holding in never grabs" test never actually touched the wall — it proved a body released the wrong way for the right side effect. Rewritten to genuinely touch the wall first, then release, before asserting no grab follows. Four: STANCE.ledgeGrab's own comment in 35-rig.js claimed the existing 1500-tick figure-drive sweep validated the pose — it structurally cannot, since that sweep runs against flatWorld, which has no wall that ever ends into a ledge. Comment corrected; a dedicated test added that drives a real ledgeGrab on a real constructed world and poses it directly.

The standalone probe script written to hand-verify detectLedge() before wiring it in used TILE (the tile-kind enum) in a pixel arithmetic expression where CFG.TILE (16, the tile size) was needed, producing NaN. Caught immediately by the probe's own nonsensical null results, not assumed correct. Never touched anything shipped.

What was deliberately not done here: no ledge-to-ledge chaining or wall-to-wall climbing beyond one catch-climb-or-drop cycle — genre convention for this move is exactly that cycle. The re-grab lockout test's own precision is coupled to its test geometry, named honestly in the test's own comment rather than left implicit.

Meta progression — persistence, blueprints, +max HP (v0.2.14, D4/D8)

D8 named four purchases: "flask charges, +max HP, backpack slot, starting-loadout choice." D4 named the blueprint loop: "drop, carry, lose on death, hand in at a transition, pay to unlock into the pool. Stage 1 ships with the pool pre-unlocked, plus a debug-room toggle to enforce it." Both locked from the start; this is the first session that builds any of it.

Scope, decided explicitly — the user chose from three options. The middle ground won: D4's blueprint loop plus D8's +max HP purchase, not all four purchases and not zero. Flask charges (a consumable/heal mechanic) and a backpack slot (a capacity concept) are genuinely NEW game design with zero existing engine surface — unlike +max HP (reuses D2's own "+HP" vocabulary directly) or blueprint unlocks (target the four already-built, D9-locked weapons). Named and deliberately deferred, the same two-step discipline D11/D12 already used.

No judged panel — the one real design decision was reading D4 precisely. "Lose on death" and "hand in at a transition" are the run's own TWO endings (D1 names death as one of exactly two ways a run ends), not sequential steps of one outcome. A player whose own death ends the run never reaches a transition alive — they always lose their carry, never hand it in. Only a SURVIVING player (a boss victory with no death, or a living co-op partner) ever hands one in.

A real bug in this session's OWN test suite, caught by actually running it. The first draft of verify_meta.js assumed a player who dies AND whose death triggers the run-end would ALSO hand in their carried blueprint at that commit. Running it failed three ways — not unlocked, currency not spent, no event fired. Tracing why found the CODE was already correct: the dying player's own natural respawn clears carriedBlueprint before _commitPendingLevel() ever runs, the same tick, exactly D4's "lose on death." Rewritten around a boss-victory transition instead, plus a co-op scenario proving both outcomes at once — one player dies and loses theirs, the survivor hands in and unlocks theirs.

Currency: two numbers on purpose. run.currency (within-session, reset by beginRun()) and meta.currency (permanent, survives a restart AND a reload) both grow by the same earned amount at every commit — "total earned this session" and "current spendable wallet," not a bug.
